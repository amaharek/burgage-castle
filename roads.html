The three asks

Cars come off the N81 and fly down this road. People walk it all day, and there isn't a bin on the whole stretch.

1Speed ramps. The main one. Signs and paint won't slow anyone down here — something physical will.
2A curved mirror where you can't see what's coming when you pull out.
3Bins — litter and dog waste. Cups and cans end up in the hedges, and bagged dog waste gets left on walls because there's nowhere to put it.

Traffic calming goes on a district priority list. The more households that write in, the higher up it goes.

Step 2

Send all three

Roads and bins are different sections of the council, so they go separately and each gets its own answer. The councillors' one is what gets it onto a meeting agenda.

Step 3

Keep it going

  • Ask for a reference number, and ask where the road sits on the district's traffic calming list. That gives you something to chase.
  • Speeding itself is a Garda matter, not the council's. A call to Blessington Garda Station asking for speed checks on this stretch is worth doing alongside the emails.
  • Baltinglass Municipal District meets monthly. Once you've emailed the councillors, follow up after the next meeting and ask what came of it.
  • Take photos of the litter and the bagged dog waste, with the date. It's hard to argue with.
  • Numbers are what move this. One letter is a request; twenty from the same road is a problem they have to schedule.
